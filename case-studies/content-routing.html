Approved Content into Calendars

Offline router tests

The context behind the work

A sanitized Python sample demonstrates how approved copy can reach a dated content calendar. Client credentials and original content are excluded.

The problem to work through

Content calendars require consistent dates, field names, and approval ownership. Manual copying can introduce mismatched rows or overwrite existing content.

My contribution to the work

The sample selects a calendar row by date, merges supplied fields, and handles router errors. It includes tests for matching, merging, and error parsing.

The result and its limits

Offline tests cover the documented component behaviors. They do not prove live permissions, concurrent-edit safety, or business time savings. Connected validation remains a separate step.

AI assistance and human judgment

The routing code does not require an AI model. AI-assisted content must be reviewed before it reaches this step.

Human review and operating responsibility

The operator confirms copy approval and target dates. The sample itself does not enforce an approval workflow.

What the evidence can support

Component-tested. Six offline tests use mocked router responses. Live integration and business outcomes remain unverified.
